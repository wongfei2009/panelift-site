Support

Quick fixes for the common problems. Still stuck? Ask on GitHub, with your Mac, your iPhone or iPad, and their system versions.

Your Mac isn’t listed

  • Check that PaneLift is running on your Mac: its icon is in the menu bar. To have it start by itself, turn on Launch at login in Settings › General.
  • Put both on the same Wi-Fi network. Guest and office networks often keep devices apart: use a USB cable there.
  • Wake your Mac. A sleeping Mac can’t be reached.
  • Turn on Local Network for PaneLift: on your iPhone or iPad in Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network, and on macOS 15 or later in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network.
  • If your Mac is listed but won’t connect and its firewall is on, PaneLift’s Settings › General shows a warning with the Terminal command that unblocks it.

Pairing

Each iPhone or iPad pairs once. Tap your Mac, then scan the code your Mac shows or type its 12 digits. Over a USB cable it pairs by itself. Your Mac has to be unlocked.

  • No code on your Mac? Choose Pair iPhone or iPad… in the PaneLift menu, then tap your Mac again.
  • Using your Mac through Screen Sharing? Turn on Show the pairing window to screen sharing, in Settings › Devices › Advanced.
  • Lost a device? Remove it in Settings › Devices on your Mac.

No windows, or a black picture

  • No windows: turn on PaneLift in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Screen & System Audio Recording, then reopen PaneLift.
  • A black picture: your Mac’s display is asleep. Move the pointer, or press Command on an attached keyboard. To keep it on longer, change when the display turns off in System Settings › Lock Screen.

Taps and typing don’t reach your Mac

Turn on PaneLift in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility. It works right away.

The controls

Everything is in one pill floating over the picture. Drag its grip to move it; tap the grip to fold it away.

  • Switch: the Desktop or a window, your windows and your apps; its button shows what’s on screen. A window you open from your device, such as a file from Finder or a link, comes to you, and closing it takes you back. Touch and hold a window to close or minimize it.
  • Keyboard and Keys: the on-screen keyboard, and Esc, Tab, Control, Option, Command, the arrows, Delete and Return.
  • Touch or Pointer: tap where you look, or use the whole screen as a trackpad. Two fingers scroll, swipe back and forward, pinch to zoom, rotate, and double-tap to smart zoom; three fingers drag windows and select text.
  • Menus: the menu bar of the app you’re using.
  • Settings: quality, sound, clipboard, and Disconnect.

To type by voice, hold the mic at the bottom right and speak, then let go: the words are typed on your Mac. Slide left onto ✕ to cancel, tap ⏎ afterwards to press Return, and tap the chip above the mic to change language. Turn it off in your device’s settings, under Voice Typing.

On iPad, Apple Pencil works as a drawing tablet’s pen in apps that read one, such as Krita: how hard you press and how you tilt it reach your Mac.

In touch mode, pinch to zoom in, up to five times. A keyboard attached to your device types on your Mac directly, shortcuts included, and your device’s own keyboards type any language.

A window left your Mac’s screen

The window you’re using moves onto an invisible display sized for your device, so it stays sharp and keeps updating. It comes back when you switch windows, disconnect or quit. To see it on your Mac and your device at once, stream the Desktop instead.

Copy and paste

With Share clipboard on, text you copy on your Mac is ready to paste on your device. To send text the other way, tap Paste in PaneLift. Only text, and only while connected. If nothing arrives from your Mac, Settings › General says what macOS needs.

Sound (beta)

Your device plays the sound of what you’re looking at, and your Mac keeps playing it too. Turn it off with Send Audio in the PaneLift menu, or mute one device in its own settings.

Your Mac is locked

While your Mac is locked, your iPhone or iPad shows its lock screen and nothing else: the Apps list, your Mac’s menus, its sound and the clipboard wait. Type your Mac’s password from your device to unlock it, and its windows come back. While your Mac is at its login window or switched to another user, PaneLift shows none of your session, and nothing you tap or type reaches your Mac until you log in on it.

Privacy Screen

Turn on Privacy Screen in Settings › Devices on your Mac, or in your device’s settings, and your Mac’s displays go black while a device is connected. Touching your Mac’s keyboard, trackpad or mouse locks it. It’s a cover, not a lock: anyone who can unlock your Mac can still use it, and unlocking it at the Mac disconnects your device. While it’s covered, your Mac’s screen is dark and its speakers are muted (your device still hears the sound); both come back when you disconnect. If fast user switching is on, Settings › Devices offers to turn it off: from the lock screen, its Switch User leads to the login window, which your device can’t unlock.

Remote Access

Use your Mac from anywhere through your own VPN, such as Tailscale. Choose Remote Access… in the PaneLift menu and turn it on, connect your device at home once so it learns the way, then turn on the VPN on your device when you’re away. Your Mac has to stay awake: set that in System Settings › Energy, or Battery on a laptop.

The picture stutters or lags

  • Choose Optimize For › Automatic or Text, in the PaneLift menu or on your device.
  • Use a USB cable or 5 GHz Wi-Fi.

Sending a log

Choose Show Log… in Settings › General. It lists window and device names and network addresses, never what you copy, so read it before you send it.
