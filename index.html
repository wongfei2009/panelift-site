For vibe coders

Your agent keeps coding. You don’t have to sit there.

Leave Claude Code, Codex or your editor’s agent running on your Mac, and check in from the sofa, the kitchen or the train. Read what it did, answer its question, approve the change, send the next prompt.

  • Terminal keys on the keyboard. Esc, Control, Tab, the arrows, Delete and Return sit right on top of it, and typing follows your Mac’s layout.
  • Say the prompt. Hold the mic, talk, let go: the words are typed on your Mac.
  • Away from home too. Through your own VPN, such as Tailscale, with your Mac’s screen dark behind you if you turn on Privacy Screen.
  • Copy and paste both ways. An error message to your phone, a link back to your Mac.

For designers

Hand your client the iPad, not your Mac.

Your work stays on the Mac Studio, Mac mini or MacBook where it lives. Pick the one window you want to show and it fills the iPad, sharp and live, ready to pass across the table.

  • Only that window. Not your desktop, your other apps or your messages.
  • Live, not a screenshot. Change something on your Mac and they see it at once, even with the window covered on your Mac.
  • Theirs to explore. They pinch to zoom in, up to five times, or tap through your prototype.
  • Sharp lines. Optimize for Drawing or Text, and every still moment is redrawn at full detail.

For artists

Your iPad becomes your Mac’s drawing tablet.

Apple Pencil’s pressure and tilt reach your Mac as a tablet’s pen, so the brushes you know in your Mac’s drawing apps respond to how you draw. No second tablet on the desk.

  • Pressure and tilt. In apps that read a tablet’s pen, such as Krita.
  • Plug in for the quickest stroke. Over a USB cable the picture keeps 60 frames a second, with nothing in the way.
  • Your canvas, edge to edge. The window fills the iPad, and pinch, rotate and smart zoom work as on a Mac’s trackpad.

For everyone

Your whole Mac, smooth as sitting in front of it.

Apple’s own video hardware on both ends, up to 60 frames a second, and a desktop shaped like your iPhone or iPad so it fills the screen with no black bars.

  • A real trackpad. Turn the screen into one: two fingers scroll and swipe between pages, pinch and rotate, three fingers drag.
  • One pill, nothing else. Every control in one small pill you drag anywhere or fold away.
  • Your Mac’s sound, in beta.
  • Private and direct. Wi-Fi, a USB cable or your own VPN. No account, no servers, no subscription.

Get started

  1. Download PaneLift for Mac and open it. Allow Screen Recording and Accessibility when it asks.
  2. Get PaneLift on your iPhone or iPad (coming soon to the App Store).
  3. Tap your Mac and scan the code it shows. Over a USB cable, they pair by themselves.

Needs a Mac with Apple silicon on macOS 14 or later, and an iPhone or iPad on iOS 17 or iPadOS 17 or later.

Built on Sill

PaneLift starts from Sill, Noah Saffer’s open-source app, and redesigns it for the small screen: one pill instead of toolbars, a desktop in your device’s shape, keys on the keyboard, typing that follows your Mac’s layout, and simpler settings on both sides.
